E-commerce laws

Select Language

E-commerce laws in India are a mix of specific rules and existing laws covering IT, consumer rights, taxes, and FDI, with key regulations including the IT Act, 2000 (digital signatures, cyber security), the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, and the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 (transparency, grievance redressal, no pre-ticked boxes). Other crucial laws are the GST Act, 2017 for taxation, the FDI Policy (limiting inventory models for FDI), and the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 (packaging/labeling). These frameworks aim to ensure fair practices, data protection, and clear accountability for platforms and sellers

Key Laws & Regulations

Information Technology (IT) Act, 2000:

The backbone for digital transactions, covering electronic contracts, digital signatures, data security, and cybercrime prevention.

Consumer Protection Act, 2019 & E-Commerce Rules, 2020:

Mandates transparency (product details, pricing), grievance redressal within 48 hours, fair returns/refunds, explicit consent (no pre-ticked boxes), and bans unfair practices like price manipulation.

FDI Policy:

Allows 100% FDI in B2B and marketplace B2C models but restricts inventory-based B2C models.

Goods and Services Tax (GST) Act, 2017:

Regulates taxation on e-commerce sales, with platforms often responsible for tax collection and remittance.

Legal Metrology Act, 2009 & Rules:

Governs packaging, labeling, MRP, net quantity, and country of origin declarations for all goods, including online sales.

Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) 1999:

Regulates foreign exchange aspects of e-commerce.

Core Principles & Obligations

Transparency:

Full disclosure of product details, costs, origin, and seller info.

Data Privacy:

Rules around consent for data usage and security measures.

Fair Practices:

Prohibition of deceptive advertising and unfair price manipulation.

Grievance Redressal:

Platforms must address complaints within 48 hours.

Enforcement & Compliance:

E-commerce entities (domestic & foreign) must comply with these rules. Non-compliance can lead to penalties, including fines and product recalls, as seen in cases involving Amazon.
